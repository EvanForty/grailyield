Listing your own grail

  1. Connect the wallet holding the Collector Crypt pNFT.
  2. Lock it into a rental contract and set a daily rate.
  3. Collect rent each lease. Redeem the slab whenever it’s not on lease.

One slab. Three ways it ends.

A lease is a time-boxed right to show the card, nothing more. Ownership, redemption and the vaulted slab never move to the renter.

Lease runs to expiry

The window closes, the display receipt goes inactive, and the pNFT is back in the owner’s control. List it again or redeem it.

Owner ends at expiry

Owners can decline to renew at the end of any window. Rent already paid stays with the owner; renewals are never automatic.

Renter defaults

Miss an extension payment and the contract returns the pNFT to the owner on its own. Nothing to dispute, nothing to recover.

Why not just sell?

Instant buyback pays 85–93% of value and the grail is gone. Lending unlocks cash but not the card. Renting keeps both.

Built on rails that already work

GrailYield doesn’t vault, grade or ship anything. It sits on top of Collector Crypt’s custody and redemption and adds the one thing missing: a way to earn from a card without letting it go.

Buyback is an exit. A lease is a reason to keep holding.
Custody

Slabs stay in the same secured, insured vaults. The contract only locks the pNFT that represents the card.

Payments

Rent is paid up front in USDC or CARDS for the whole window and settles to the owner when the lease starts.

Receipts

Display receipts are non-transferable and can’t be sold, lent or used as collateral. They exist to be shown.

Redemption

The owner keeps redemption rights. Once a lease ends, the pNFT redeems through the normal Collector Crypt flow.

Solana

Connect Phantom, Solflare, Backpack or any Wallet Standard wallet. Contracts, receipts and pNFTs all live on Solana.
